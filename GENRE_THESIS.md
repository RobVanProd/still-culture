# Genre thesis

This document argues that STILL CULTURE is structurally novel rather than an
existing genre in a costume. It is written to be read by someone who does not
believe that, so it engages the strongest reductions rather than the
convenient ones, and it ends with the specific playtest observations that
would prove it wrong.

Everything quoted here comes from the selection record: twelve concepts across
four design lenses, eight adversarial critics (two per lens, one hunting prior
art and one hunting dominant strategies), three independent judges. The raw
concepts are in `docs/_concepts_raw.json`. The critic verdicts and judge
rankings are in the workflow journal. Where a critic said something sharper
than we would have said it ourselves, the critic is quoted.

---

## 1. The structural claim

### The shape of the decision

At any instant the player holds a belief about the hidden state of a living
field, with some uncertainty. Three kinds of action are available.

| Kind | Examples | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Actuators | thermal ring, nutrient drip, shear | nothing informational; effects are coarse, delayed and ambiguous |
| Free channels | the hum, meniscus behaviour at the dish edge, particle coherence | nothing; always on, always ambiguous, never decisive alone |
| Instruments | fluorescence (bleaches a disc), aspirate (removes mass permanently) | the graded artefact itself |

The decision the player makes, second by second, is whether to convert
artefact into knowledge. That is the whole game. Four properties give it its
shape, and while none is individually unprecedented, the composite is not in
the library.

**1. The price of information is denominated in the score.** Not time, not
money, not risk exposure, not opportunity cost. The thing consumed by looking
is the same continuous field the assay grades at the end. Fluorescence costs a
disc of the culture roughly ninety seconds of growth; aspirate punches a hole
the medium must heal around.

**2. The price is certain and immediate; the return is uncertain and
conditional.** A probe pays only if its answer changes a subsequent action.
Information that confirms what you already believed is a pure loss — you have
bleached a region and behaved exactly as you would have anyway. The concept's
own description of novice play is precisely this: a dish that matches the
stencil at 80% and fails viability, "having spent all eight charges on
questions whose answers changed no action they took."

**3. Restraint is on the scoresheet, not merely rewarded by absence of loss.**
Two numbers at the end: what the culture became, and how much of it you burned
finding out. This is the difference between a mechanic and a genre. A game
where looking is expensive teaches you to economise. A game where *not
looking* is graded makes the epistemic posture itself the object of play.

**4. The correct amount of looking is situational rather than
skill-dependent.** It does not converge with practice to "never" or "always";
it depends on the dish in front of you. One judge put the point better than we
can: the decision "is live in the first thirty seconds and it never resolves,
because it is situational rather than skill-dependent."

Property 4 is the one that makes this a form rather than a puzzle. It is also
the one most at risk, and section 4 is about defending it.

### Why that shape is not in the existing library

Management, colony and city-building games are built on a shared and unspoken
grammar: **observation is free and unlimited, and scarcity lives in
materials.** You may inspect any building, any citizen, any production chain,
as often as you like, at no cost. What you cannot do is afford everything.

STILL CULTURE inverts exactly that. Materials are not scarce; the culture
grows on its own and mostly self-corrects. What is scarce is state knowledge,
and its price is levied against the score. One critic named the consequence:

> "In every colony sim, city builder and organism sim ever shipped, looking is
> free and unlimited and scarcity is material; making observation itself the
> costed act changes what decisions are *about* from allocation to
> epistemics, which is why the 'normal management sim with one unusual
> mechanic' objection does not quite land."

That is the claim, stated precisely: **the decisions are epistemic rather than
allocative.** A management sim asks where you should spend. This asks what it
is worth to stop being uncertain, knowing the answer is billed to the thing
you are trying to save.

## 2. The reductions that were attempted, and why each failed

Two adversarial critics reviewed STILL CULTURE, one from each hostile lens.
Both were tasked with finding the game it reduces to. Both failed, and both
recorded a keep — the only concept of the twelve to receive two keeps. Their
prior-art searches are worth reproducing in full, because the argument is only
as good as the strongest reduction it survives.

| Reduction | What the critic conceded it shares | Why it does not land |
|---|---|---|
| Fog of war | Blind play; information arrives by moving through the world | Fog lifts for free. Movement is the cost, and the map is not damaged by being seen. Nothing is graded on how much fog you lifted. |
| Duskers (2016) | "The closest released thing, and it is close": blind, ambient and degraded channels, motion tracker and audio doing the work, looking harder means exposing something you cannot afford | "Duskers' looking costs risk not substrate." Risk is a probability of a loss elsewhere and it can be survived. Here the loss is certain, immediate, and inside the graded object. |
| Theme Hospital / Two Point Hospital / Project Hospital | The agony itself: "a diagnosis confidence percentage, tests that cost time the patient does not have, and the option to act at 60% and kill them" — which "shipped thirty years ago" | "Theme Hospital charges you time and money." The diagnosis machine does not deform the patient. There is no second score for how much you tested, and the patient is a scalar with a timer, not a continuous field the instrument bleaches. |
| Hardspace: Shipbreaker (2022) | A tool that damages the object you are scored on | "Hardspace charges value but the cutter is not an instrument." The cutter is the actuator. Damage there is clumsiness with your working tool, and cutting is compulsory. Here the damaging device is the *sensor*, and declining to use it is an available — often correct — move. |
| Cell Lab | The petri dish with goals and failure states | Supplies the frame, not the decision. Looking is free. |
| Lenia, Powder Toy, reaction-diffusion pieces (Cell Lab, Ecosystem, Equilinox) | The entire look | "The toy half the pitch correctly dismisses." No goal, no failure, no cost to looking. They "supply the look and nothing else, as the pitch concedes." |
| Alien: Isolation, Phasmophobia | "Using the instrument endangers you" | It endangers *you*, probabilistically and recoverably. It does not consume the artefact you are graded on. |
| Dishonored (Chaos system) | Conduct visibly degrading the thing you are graded in | Chaos accrues from violence — an actuator choice. Nothing there charges you for *knowing*. |
| Black & White (2001) | An un-commandable ward that self-corrects | The player role, not the decision. Observation is free. |
| Creatures (1996) | A hidden genome you cannot command, only influence, carried across sessions by breeding, with drifted constants in the offspring | **This one lands** — see below. |

The judges' formulation is the compact version of the whole table, and it is
the sentence to hand a hostile reader first:

> Every management sim makes observation free and unlimited and puts scarcity
> in materials; here the act of measuring permanently disfigures the artefact
> you are graded on, and the disfigurement is scored separately.

Both critics arrived independently at the same negative result. The
prior-art critic:

> "What I cannot find prior art for is the actual central decision:
> observation that damages the thing you are being scored on preserving, with
> restraint explicitly on the scoresheet. [...] This is the one of the three
> where the reduction does not land."

And the second:

> "But I cannot name a released game where the act of *measuring* permanently
> disfigures the artefact you are graded on, with the disfigurement as an
> explicit second score. [...] The composite is not in the library."

### The reduction that did land

Creatures. The critic was blunt: "the strain-lineage pitch is the norn genome
with scars added." A hidden genome, influence rather than command, persistence
across sessions by breeding, drifted constants in the offspring — the lineage
subsystem is a 1996 design with an inheritance rule added to it.

We accept this without argument, and the design response is to remove the
subsystem from the prototype rather than defend it. The same critic:

> "Cut persistence from the prototype entirely; if the ten minutes are not
> gripping without a lineage to grow, the lineage is compensating for a hollow
> core and you want to know that in week one."

This matters to the thesis in a way that is worth being explicit about. The
novelty claim is about the ten-minute session, not about the meta. If the
session needs a progression system to be interesting, the thesis is false and
we would rather discover that in week one than in month four. Scar inheritance
remains the most interesting long-arc idea in the record — a lineage you
interrogated heavily becomes a lineage that responds badly to interrogation —
but it is a reward for a core that works, not a component of the argument that
the core is new.

## 3. The novelty does not depend on the theme

Both judges who addressed it treated this as the decisive test, and used the
same phrasing: "nothing structural here depends on the biology theme, which is
the mark of a real form rather than a costume."

The test is easy to state. **A costume is a familiar form that, when the
costume is removed, reveals a game that already exists. A form is one that,
when reskinned, produces something that still does not exist.**

Strip the biology from STILL CULTURE and the requirements that remain are:

- a continuous hidden state that develops on its own and mostly self-corrects;
- coarse actuators whose effects are delayed and ambiguous;
- free ambient channels that are informative but never individually decisive;
- instruments that resolve uncertainty by consuming the graded object;
- irreversible differentiation, so that waiting purchases certainty with
  authority;
- two scores, one of which is restraint.

Nothing in that list says "cells". Three reskins that preserve every line:

**Destructive excavation.** Archaeology is literally this: digging is
irreversible, stratigraphy is consumed by being read, and the site is graded
both on what you reconstructed and on how much undisturbed context you spent
doing it. The free channels are surface survey and geophysics — always
available, always ambiguous.

**Painting conservation.** Pigment analysis removes pigment. Raking light and
the sound of the surface under a dry brush are free and equivocal. Score: the
restored painting, and the milligrams taken to restore it.

**Instrument restoration.** Every measurement of a wooden soundbox removes
wood. You are graded on how it sounds and on how much of the original body
survives.

Each of those is a game that does not exist, which is the evidence. Now run
the same test on the concepts that lost. The record's verdict on THE UNEVEN
HOUR is that its core verb is "Creeper World's terrain vocabulary with the
substrate relabelled" — remove the costume and you arrive at a shipped game.
SLOW MATCH scored 151 and was rejected on exactly this ground; one judge:
"the novelty is thematic, not structural — even its defending critic conceded
the thinking shape is old."

What the reskin test proves is narrow and should not be overstated: it proves
the novelty is not decorative. It does not prove the form is any good.
Sections 4 and 5 are about that.

## 4. The two ways it could still collapse

Both critics kept the concept and both named a way it dies. These are the most
important passages in the record, and they are reproduced without softening.

### Collapse 1 — the fixed opening

> "The fixed opening, and the design states it out loud: the expert 'probes
> once, early, at the single place where the latent binary branches their
> plan.' If the latent binary is what risk (2) requires it to be — lethal,
> common, and truly unreadable from ambient cues — then a single deterministic
> probe at the branch point is correct every single session, and 'do I look'
> stops being a live decision by roughly session five. Everything after is
> execution with three coarse actuators. The design has no mechanism forcing
> the *location or timing* of the branch point to itself be uncertain, which
> is the thing that would keep the decision alive. Fix it before the hum,
> before the render, before anything."

This kills property 4 from section 1. If the right amount of looking converges
to a memorised opening, the decision is not situational, and what remains is
a pretty execution game.

**Decision: the branch point is a function of the culture's own growth.**

The latent binary manifests at whichever lobe actually becomes dominant, and
which lobe becomes dominant is influenced by the player's own shaping actions.
"Probe the branch point" therefore requires first knowing where the branch
point will be, which is itself a read of the free channels — the skill the
game is about. Multiple binaries with staggered, growth-dependent onset, so
that no single moment answers everything and no single probe is ever
sufficient.

The reasoning is that the branch location must remain **inferable but not
memorable**. That is a narrow target and both obvious alternatives miss it:

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Randomise the branch location uniformly per dish | An unconditionally random location is not inferable at all. Probing becomes a lottery, the free channels stop paying, and the skill the game is built on evaporates. Uncertainty is necessary; *unstructured* uncertainty is fatal. |
| Add more strain families | Content, not structure. More families postpone memorisation instead of preventing it, and the cost is linear in families while the benefit decays. |
| Randomise only the binary's value, as originally specified | This is the state the critic condemned. The value being unknown is what makes the probe worth taking; the *location* being knowable in advance is what makes the probe schedulable. |

Making the branch location depend on growth also has a property none of the
alternatives has: the player's own interventions move it. Shaping the culture
changes where the question will be asked. That converts a scheduling problem
into a live one.

### Collapse 2 — stalling for free information

> "The strong one is stalling for free information. Ambient channels are free
> and they become decisive late — the pitch says so outright, that the two
> outcomes have different beat frequencies 'once they are far enough along.'
> So time is the only price of free information, and the optimal policy is to
> refuse commitment: keep the mass generic and undifferentiated, take no
> shaping action that a latent binary could invert, and commit hard in the last
> ninety seconds once the binaries have resolved for free. [...] Either way the
> game flattens to a fixed schedule rather than a read."

And the underlying worry, which is the sharpest single line either critic
wrote:

> "Underneath both: a substrate that self-corrects and punishes intervention
> has a famous optimum, which is minimal intervention. 'Most of the culture
> self-corrects' is a sentence that should worry you."

**Decision: the medium differentiates irreversibly, and actuator authority
decays as it does.**

The longer the culture grows, the less plastic it becomes and the less the
actuators can move it. A thermal ring that redirects a lobe at t=60s barely
deflects it at t=400s. Waiting is therefore not free: it buys certainty with
authority. Late commitment is weak commitment.

Two things about this answer matter more than the mechanism itself.

*It is physically motivated rather than imposed.* An arbitrary late-action
penalty would produce identical numbers and the wrong lesson. A player who is
punished by a timer learns that the game dislikes patience, which is a
catastrophic thing to teach in a game whose subject is restraint. A player who
watches a lobe harden learns that the culture is becoming itself and will
shortly stop listening. The first reads as an authored rule; the second reads
as a fact about the thing in the dish.

*It must be perceptible while it happens, not only at the assay.* If declining
authority is a hidden multiplier, it is an arbitrary timer wearing a costume.
The same input must visibly and audibly do less, and the player must be able
to feel the plasticity going. This is a build requirement, not a tuning
parameter.

Note also what this answer does *not* do: it does not make waiting wrong.
Restraint remains frequently correct, and "most of the culture self-corrects"
remains true. What changes is that waiting has a price denominated in
something other than the clock, so the fixed schedule the critic predicted has
no reason to be fixed.

One judge observed that the two collapses are in tension with each other — "if
stalling wins, the fixed opening does not" — which is a reason to think both
are tuning problems rather than logical impossibilities, and a reason to be
suspicious of any fix that solves one by making the other worse. The
irreversible-differentiation answer is chosen partly because it does not: it
penalises late commitment without making early probing more schedulable.

### Collapse 3 — the player cannot run a controlled experiment

The prior-art critic considered this the deepest problem, deeper than either
dominant strategy, and flagged that the design had not noticed it:

> "The player cannot run a controlled experiment. Actuators are coarse, latency
> is forty seconds, the field is continuous with many causes, every dish is a
> different draw of latent binaries, and probing — the only direct read — is
> scored against you. So when something happens the player cannot attribute it:
> was that the nutrient drip, the variant, or would it have branched anyway? A
> player who fails cannot form a theory, and cannot test a theory if they form
> one. That is not mystery, that is opacity, and it is the thing that makes
> people put a game down while still calling it beautiful."

**Decision: an after-action trace.**

During the run the player gets nothing extra. Afterwards they can scrub the
run and see their interventions laid against what the field actually did. This
teaches attribution without handing out free information during play, which is
the constraint that rules out most of the obvious answers.

It is also nearly free to build, for a reason that comes out of
`ARCHITECTURE.md` rather than out of design: the simulation is a pure function
of (seed, tick, input stream), fixed-step at 120 Hz, with every random draw
from a named stream. A run is therefore exactly replayable from its input log,
and the trace is a replay with the input events drawn on top. Determinism was
adopted for verification; attribution is a second use of the same property.

The alternative the critic proposed was the **sister dish**: a parallel culture
from the same seed that the player may butcher freely. Two of the three judges
independently asked for it to be A/B'd against the pure version. It is not
rejected — it remains the leading candidate if the trace proves insufficient —
but it is not the first answer, for three reasons. It doubles the screen and
halves the attention available to the free channels, which are the game's
actual subject. It restores the controlled experiment *during* the run, which
reintroduces free information late, the thing collapse 2 is about. And it
changes the central question from "do I look" to "do I look here or there",
which the critic considered an improvement and which we consider a different
game. If the trace fails, that different game is where we go.

### Collapse 4 — restraint cannot be taught by demonstration

> "The central lesson — most of the culture self-corrects, so do nothing —
> cannot be taught by demonstration, because doing nothing and doing the
> *right* nothing look identical on screen and identical in the log. The player
> who correctly withholds and the player who was confused both did the same
> thing."

**Decision: the end-of-run assay names waste explicitly.**

Doing nothing and doing the right nothing are identical during the run. They
are not identical in the report. The assay reports probes that changed no
subsequent action — the operational definition being a probe not followed by
an actuator input that differs from the trajectory the player was already on,
within a window. This is an approximation and we know it: a probe whose answer
correctly told the player to keep doing what they were doing is real
information and would be scored as waste. The window length and the definition
of "differs" are unsolved and will be tuned against recorded sessions. The
report is honest about this, or it will teach the wrong lesson in the other
direction.

What the report cannot do is name the *inverse* error — the probe you should
have taken and did not. That is unattributable in principle, since we cannot
know what you would have done with the answer. The report can say what a
probe would have revealed at the moments the trace shows you hesitating, which
is weaker and is the best available.

## 5. Falsification plan

The concept's own kill criteria are two sentences:

> "It is dead if testers report the ten minutes felt like waiting, or if a
> policy of never probing scores as well as a considered one — the first means
> the free channels are not tense enough, the second means the latent state is
> not doing its job."

The critics added several more. All of them are cheap, and most run before any
human tester is involved. The prototype is one strain family, one target
morphology, three actuators, one probe, a ten-minute clock and two scores; the
first critic's assessment was that "if never-probing scores as well as
considered play you will know in a day."

| # | The thesis is wrong if we observe | How it is measured | Kill threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The ten minutes feel like waiting | Unprompted post-session free response, first-time testers | A majority volunteer waiting, boredom or idleness without being asked |
| 2 | Never probing scores as well as considered play | Scripted zero-probe agent vs scripted considered agent over the same 200 seeds | Zero-probe median combined score within noise of considered play. No testers needed; runs headless |
| 3 | The opening probe converges to a memorised one | Log first-probe time and position per session, in **dish coordinates** and in the **culture's own frame** | Both converge by session five. If the growth-dependent branch works, position converges in the culture's frame while remaining scattered in dish coordinates and in time. If dish coordinates converge too, the fix failed |
| 4 | Stalling dominates | Log the time of first committing action — one large enough to change the reachable morphology set | First-commit time drifts *later* with player experience and late committers score higher. If irreversible differentiation works, experienced players commit earlier |
| 5 | Multi-channel reading collapses to one channel | Ablation groups: mute the hum; freeze the meniscus; decohere the particle layer | Only the hum ablation moves scores. The critic's prediction is explicit: if so, the game is "listen for the beat, act, which is a single-channel reaction game with a pretty renderer" |
| 6 | Shape-match scores are unpredictable | Ask each tester to predict their own shape-match band before the assay runs | Most testers mis-band. "I thought that looked right" is corrosive to a game whose premise is trusting your own read |
| 7 | Coarse actuators read as broken controls | Count sessions where a tester says a control did the opposite of what they expected and never revises it to "it was the variant" | Frequent and unrevised. A latent inversion that is never understood as an inversion is indistinguishable from a bug |
| 8 | The audio is not honestly substitutable | Score distributions with sound on vs sound off | A large gap. A player on laptop speakers is then playing a worse game with no way to discover that |
| 9 | Strain drift resets intuition rather than compounding it | Session-over-session score trend on a drifted lineage vs a fresh one | Drifted lineages perform no better than fresh ones. The critic's phrasing: the player "has not deepened, they have had their furniture moved" |
| 10 | There is only one right way to play | Cluster the finished dishes of winning runs | Winning dishes converge on one look. The concept predicts two divergent winning styles — blind farmers working by ear and instrument-first players buying certainty early — "and their dishes look nothing alike." One cluster means the decision has a single correct answer, which is property 4 falsified |

Observations 2, 3, 4 and 5 are the load-bearing ones, because they test the
four properties of section 1 directly. Observation 10 is the most informative
positive signal available and costs nothing to collect.

### What would not falsify the thesis

- Players losing, or finding the game hard. Difficulty is not the claim.
- Players disagreeing about strategy. That is evidence *for* property 4.
- Low absolute scores. The thesis concerns the shape of the decision, not its
  calibration.
- Testers describing the game as slow. Slow and "felt like waiting" are
  different reports, and only the second is a kill; the distinction has to be
  made from unprompted language rather than from a Likert item.

## What this thesis does not claim

- **Not that the look is new.** Reaction-diffusion is decades old and the
  visual debt to Lenia, Powder Toy and Cell Lab is total. The record's own
  words: those pieces "supply the look and nothing else, as the pitch
  concedes."
- **Not that the agony is unprecedented.** Theme Hospital shipped
  probe-more-or-commit-on-a-weak-read in 1997. What is unprecedented is where
  the bill is sent.
- **Not that the lineage meta is new.** It is Creatures with scars, and it is
  cut from the prototype for that reason.
- **Not that the design is finished.** Four collapses are named above, and
  three of the four answers are unbuilt at the time of writing. This document
  records what was decided and why, not what has been demonstrated.

unanimous result, and the only concept in the dossier to receive a keep from
both of its critics. Every other concept that received a keep from one critic
received a kill or a wounding from the other.

Ideas the judges asked to have transplanted rather than discarded: HEARSAY's
leash ending (finishing the season alive with two hundred motes sitting still
waiting for you instead of foraging), LONGBODY's DelayNode, ARREARS' "the gap
between you and the ghost is the interest on the loan", and RESIDUAL's causal
debt campaign. None of them is in STILL CULTURE's scope and none should be
smuggled in.

### A note on the record

One judge's rationale states that "four critics across two lenses tried to
reduce it". The journal shows two critic verdicts naming STILL CULTURE, both
keeps. The stronger claim — that four critics attempted the reduction — is not
supported by the record and is not relied on anywhere in this document. Two
independent hostile reviews, both failing to reduce it, is the actual
evidence, and it is still the strongest prior-art result in the dossier.
